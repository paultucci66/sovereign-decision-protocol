# Your Company Doesn't Have a Data Problem

## It Has a Memory Problem

Your ERP remembers the transaction.

Your CRM remembers the customer.

The people who left remember why.

The organization's judgment is scattered across SharePoint, Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow, Jira, Slack, Teams, email, AI chats, meeting notes, legal documents, whiteboards, the back of an envelope, napkins at the country club, and in people's heads.

The organization retained the transaction.

It lost the decision.

Organizational judgment is the most valuable asset your company creates.

Today, almost none of it survives in a computable form.

That is not a data problem.

It is an architecture problem.

AI did not create it.

AI exposed it.

Every AI system eventually runs into the same questions.

Why was this action taken?

Who authorized it?

Under what policy?

Based on what evidence?

What changed afterward?

What did the organization learn?

Most enterprise systems were not designed to answer those questions.

They record state.

They record activity.

They record transactions.

They rarely preserve judgment.

That distinction matters.

Transactions tell us what happened.

Decisions explain why it happened.

Authority explains who was allowed to make it happen.

Those are different things.

For forty years, the enterprise has standardized almost everything.

Networks.

Identity.

Payments.

Messaging.

APIs.

Now AI.

But we never standardized the decision itself.

That may be the missing enterprise primitive.

Not another application.

Not another workflow.

Not another governance dashboard.

A protocol.

A common representation for organizational judgment.

Portable.

Verifiable.

Vendor neutral.

That is the motivation behind the Sovereign Decision Protocol.

SDP is an open protocol for representing consequential decisions.

Nothing more.

Nothing less.

It intentionally does not define workflow, AI models, storage, policy engines, dashboards, or orchestration.

Those belong to products.

The protocol defines the medium of exchange.

The product value lives in the Decision Control Plane.

That separation is intentional.

And clean.

Protocols succeed when they remain simple.

Products succeed by making them useful.

HTTP did not monetize the web.

Browsers, servers, CDNs, and platforms did.

OAuth did not monetize identity.

Identity platforms did.

SDP should work the same way.

The protocol should be small enough to adopt.

The control plane should be powerful enough to matter.

The enterprise already owns the what.

The who and the why are where the value lives.

Organizations do not become learning organizations by collecting more data.

They become learning organizations by preserving judgment.

That is the premise.

The draft specification is public.

Read it.

Challenge it.

Improve it.

Standards are built through scrutiny, not proclamation.
