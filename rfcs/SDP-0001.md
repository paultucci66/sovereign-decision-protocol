# SDP-0001: Sovereign Decision Protocol Overview

Status: Draft

Version: 0.1

Author: Paul Tucci

License: Apache 2.0

## Abstract

The Sovereign Decision Protocol (SDP) is an open, vendor-neutral protocol for representing consequential decisions.

SDP defines a portable Decision Object that records identity references, authority, policy, context, evidence, decision disposition, outcome, memory, and integrity metadata.

The protocol is intended to support interoperable decision governance across humans, AI systems, applications, and organizations.

## 1. Motivation

Organizations rely on many systems of record.

They have systems for customers, employees, finances, assets, tickets, documents, and identity.

They rarely have a system of record for decisions.

As AI agents, automated workflows, and distributed human review become more common, organizations need a standard mechanism to answer:

- Who acted?
- Were they authorized?
- Which policies applied?
- What evidence was considered?
- What decision was made?
- What action occurred?
- What was learned?

SDP provides a common representation for those answers.

## 2. Scope

SDP defines how consequential decisions are represented.

SDP includes:

- A Decision Object model
- References to identity, authority, policy, context, and evidence
- Materiality metadata
- Decision disposition
- Outcome and memory references
- Integrity and verification metadata
- Versioning and exchange semantics

## 3. Non-Goals

SDP does not define:

- Identity providers
- Authentication mechanisms
- Workflow engines
- User interfaces
- AI models
- Prompting systems
- Policy engines
- Storage engines
- Document repositories
- Internet retrieval
- Vector databases
- Application execution

Those concerns belong to implementations, applications, or Decision Control Planes.

## 4. Definitions

### Consequential Decision

A decision whose outcome may materially affect an organization, individual, stakeholder, obligation, asset, customer, policy, legal exposure, safety condition, financial result, or strategic direction.

Materiality is contextual and SHOULD be defined by organizational policy.

### Decision Object

The portable, auditable, verifiable representation of a consequential decision.

The Decision Object is the atomic unit of SDP.

### Decision Control Plane

A product or implementation layer that orchestrates consequential decisions by coordinating identity, authority, policy, context, evidence, AI, human review, decision publication, memory, and analytics.

The DCP is not the protocol.

It implements or uses the protocol.

### Application

A system that executes domain-specific work and may produce or consume SDP Decision Objects.

## 5. Design Axioms

### 5.1 Identity Is Not Authority

Authentication proves who a principal is.

Authority determines what that principal may decide.

SDP SHALL NOT require a specific identity provider.

### 5.2 Representation Is Not Execution

SDP records decisions.

It does not execute them.

### 5.3 The Decision Is the Source

Operational systems may record state changes.

Decision Objects preserve the authority, rationale, context, and intent behind those changes.

### 5.4 Context Is Referenced, Not Embedded

Context and evidence SHOULD be referenced with verifiable metadata rather than embedded by default.

### 5.5 Materiality Is Contextual

Organizations determine which decisions are consequential.

SDP provides the representation once a decision is material.

## 6. The Five Proofs

Every consequential decision SHOULD support five proofs:

| Proof | Question |
|-------|----------|
| Identity | Who acted? |
| Authority | Were they authorized? |
| Governance | Which rules applied? |
| Decision | What was decided and done? |
| Memory | What was learned? |

## 7. Layered Architecture

```text
Applications
        |
        | SDP Decision Requests / Decision Objects
        v
Decision Control Plane
        |
        | SDP
        v
Decision Objects
```

Applications execute work.

The Decision Control Plane orchestrates decisions.

SDP standardizes the Decision Object.

## 8. Authority Model

SDP separates identity from authority.

The authority model may use concepts similar to public key infrastructure:

- Trust anchors
- Delegated authority
- Authority credentials
- Decision signatures
- Revocation
- Time-bound authority
- Chain of authority

SDP does not require any particular PKI implementation.

The protocol records the authority basis for a decision and enables verification by implementations.

## 9. Context and Evidence

A Decision Control Plane may retrieve context from documents, file systems, enterprise applications, APIs, prior decisions, or internet resources.

SDP does not define how retrieval occurs.

The Decision Object records the context and evidence that were considered, preferably by reference with source, timestamp, version, and integrity metadata.

## 10. Interoperability

The objective of SDP is interoperability.

Different products, applications, and organizations should be able to exchange Decision Objects without sharing the same software implementation.

The protocol should remain small and stable so implementations can innovate above it.
